# Synthetic dataset profile (GEN-001..006, T012)

Generated by `infra/seed/generate.py` (seed=42), verified against the local dev Postgres after a
full `make seed` run. Every number below is a real query result or a direct read of
`infra/seed/ground_truth/*.json`, not an estimate — see "How to reproduce" at the bottom.

## Headline numbers vs. GEN-001

| Quantity | GEN-001 target | Actual | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Branches | 3 | 3 | Downtown Motors, Westside Auto, Airport Branch |
| Vehicle models | ~25 | 22 | rebalanced across segments — see ADR-021 |
| History | 156 weeks (3 yrs) | 156 weeks | 2023-07-17 → 2026-07-08 |
| Orders | ~4-8k | 15,202 | **deliberate deviation, see ADR-021** |
| Customers | ~6-10k | 6,374 | |
| Leads | ~6-10k | 6,000 | |

**Order volume (15,202) is ~1.9x GEN-001's stated upper bound.** This is not a bug: getting the
smooth and erratic SBC classes to ≥10 series each requires a base demand rate that is inherently
far more expensive, in total-volume terms, than intermittent/lumpy's — a mathematical property of
the NB-GLM's zero-probability, not a tuning miss. `docs/adr/021-synthetic-demand-volume-vs-sbc-coverage.md`
has the full calibration trace and the reasoning for prioritizing GEN-002's four-class coverage
requirement over GEN-001's approximate volume figure.

## SBC demand-class coverage (SC-002)

66 (model, branch) series, classified by `dealeriq_analytics.sbc.classify()` (ADI=1.32, CV²=0.49):

| Class | Series | Target segment |
|---|---|---|
| Smooth | 13 | Economy/Compact |
| Erratic | 10 | Sedan/Mid-size |
| Intermittent | 28 | SUV/Truck, Commercial/Van (spillover) |
| Lumpy | 15 | Luxury/Sports |

All four classes clear the ≥10 floor. Intermittent is over-represented (28) because it's the
"default" outcome for any series that doesn't hit one of the three parameter-tuned target zones —
harmless, since the requirement is a floor, not a target distribution.

## Lead funnel: 6,000 leads, 2,502 won (41.7%), 3,498 lost

### Conversion by channel (Customer.source_channel)

| Channel | Leads | Won | Win rate | Ground-truth coefficient |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Referral | 1,190 | 544 | 45.7% | +0.8 |
| Web form | 1,222 | 534 | 43.7% | 0.0 (baseline) |
| Phone | 1,229 | 509 | 41.4% | -0.2 |
| Walk-in | 1,167 | 469 | 40.2% | +0.3 |
| Social ad | 1,192 | 446 | 37.4% | -0.5 |

The two largest-magnitude coefficients (referral +0.8, social_ad -0.5) land exactly where
expected — highest and lowest win rate respectively. Walk-in and phone are swapped relative to
their coefficient ordering (+0.3 vs -0.2): this is a **marginal** (univariate) breakdown that
doesn't control for the other five features in the logit (budget, segment, days-since-creation,
interaction count, RFM, season), so two closely-spaced coefficients can show a noisy marginal
ordering even though the underlying effect is correctly signed. The actual validation for this
(SC-003) is a multivariate logistic regression that recovers all 13 ground-truth coefficient
signs exactly — see `packages/analytics/tests/test_lead_generator.py`. This table is descriptive,
not a substitute for that test.

`Customer.source_channel` is set from the *acquiring lead's* simulated channel for newly-created
customers (an explicit fix during T012 — without it, this column was statistically independent
of Lead.status in the database, even though the generator deliberately makes channel a real
driver of the won/lost outcome).

## Injected anomalies (GEN-004)

25 ground-truth points in `infra/seed/ground_truth/anomalies.json`: 19 demand spikes, 1 demand
drop, 5 natural near-miss points (not injected — see below). Within plan.md's 15-25-shock range,
though skewed toward spikes (the injection loop accepts the first shock type that verifies for a
given series and moves on, so a spike being found first is common — no attempt was made to force
an even split, since GEN-004 only asks for shocks "sized so the implied z-score lands in the 5-8
range", not a specific spike/drop ratio).

Every injected point's `implied_z` field is the **independently re-decomposed** (fresh STL fit on
the post-injection series) modified z-score, not a prediction — see
`packages/analytics/src/dealeriq_analytics/generator/anomalies.py` for why that distinction
mattered (STL's `robust=True` mode is numerically degenerate on this dataset's 3-cycle shape;
`robust=False` is used instead, documented in that module).

Sample (first 3 of 19 spikes):

| Series | Week | Type | Expected | Injected | Verified z |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| sedan:Ford:Fusion \| Downtown Motors | 90 | spike | 20 | 19* | 7.04 |
| sedan:Toyota:Camry \| Downtown Motors | 95 | spike | 10 | 14 | 6.70 |
| sedan:Nissan:Altima \| Downtown Motors | 63 | drop | 2 | 0 | -5.01 |

\* `expected_value` is the *clean, pre-injection* draw at that week (here 20), and `injected_value`
(19) is what actually landed in the series after solving for the target z against that series'
STL residual stats — for weeks where the series was already unusually high, "expected" and
"injected" can end up close in absolute terms even though the *classification* (via a fresh STL
fit on the final series) is unambiguously anomalous. The 5 near-miss points (implied z in [2, 3),
deliberately left unmodified) exist so Phase 5's anomaly detector has real "should NOT flag this"
cases to validate its false-positive rate against (QA-003b), not just true positives.

## Injected pricing errors (GEN-004, other half)

15 ground-truth points in `infra/seed/ground_truth/pricing_errors.json`. Each forces
`negotiated_price` on one real order down to
`list_price * (1 - (historical_discount_fraction_mean + 5 * historical_discount_fraction_std))`
for that order's specific vehicle model (k_inject=5 vs. DS-ANO-003's detector default k=3) —
grouped by model, not globally, so each injected error is judged against that model's own real
discount distribution (mean ≈ 2.8-3.0%, std ≈ 2.6-3.4% across the sampled models here).

Spread across 4 of 5 segments (economy 5, sedan 5, suv_truck 2, luxury 2, commercial 1 — no
strict quota was enforced, just >=5 historical orders per model to compute stable statistics):

| Order (model) | List price | Historical discount (μ, σ) | Original price | Injected price |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| economy:Nissan:Sentra | $19,789.99 | 2.9%, 2.9% | $18,488.95 | $16,392.83 |
| suv_truck:Ford:F-150 | $44,491.66 | 3.0%, 3.1% | $43,825.68 | $36,315.36 |
| commercial:Ram:ProMaster | $44,237.54 | 2.9%, 3.4% | $41,896.22 | $35,377.81 |

Cross-checked directly against Postgres for the first row: `orders.negotiated_price` for that
order ID is exactly $16,392.83, and `orders.discount` ($3,397.16) equals
`list_price - negotiated_price` to the cent — the injected value actually landed in the
database, not just the ground-truth JSON.

Unlike demand shocks, an injected pricing error's `order_events` trail reflects the order's
*original* (pre-injection) discount fraction — e.g., a 3% discount that auto-approved without a
`pending_approval` step can still end up with an anomalously deep final price. This is
deliberate, not an oversight: a price that changed after approval without re-triggering the
approval workflow is exactly the kind of thing DS-ANO-003's detector should flag as suspicious.

## How to reproduce

```
make seed          # infra up, migrations, then infra/seed/generate.py
```

Deterministic given seed=42 (`GeneratorConfig`'s default): re-running against a freshly-reset DB
reproduces every number in this document exactly (`packages/analytics/tests/test_demand_generator.py::test_generate_demand_is_deterministic_given_a_seed`
and friends cover this at the pure-stats layer; SC-005 in `specs/004-synthetic-data/spec.md`
covers the full pipeline).
